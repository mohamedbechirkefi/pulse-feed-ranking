# Limitations (living document; items 1 to 11 written at the freeze, 2026-11-24, before any confirmatory result; items 12 to 16 added in M3, 2026-11-25, after the confirmatory results)

1. **Simulation only.** Users and items are synthetic. Results hold only within the generative assumptions of `research/protocol.md`. They are not evidence about real people or any real platform.
2. **No claims about commercial systems.** PULSE does not claim that any platform uses these rankers or produces these effects.
3. **No measurement of addiction or wellbeing.** Attention is represented only by a position-based examination probability. Nothing here measures compulsive use.
4. **Drift is an assumption.** The drifting preference model encodes reinforcement by construction; EXP-002 measures how rankers interact with that assumption, not whether people drift.
5. **Narrow operationalisations.** "User control" is responsiveness of slates to explicit dislikes; it is not human agency. "Calibration" follows a KL definition between latent preference and exposure.
6. **Simple rankers.** No learned embedding model, no deep or RL ranker beyond LinUCB.
7. **Expected primary direction.** The primary contrast is expected by construction; the informative parts are effect size, trade-offs and the sensitivity analysis.
8. **Licence.** The repository is source-available under a non-commercial licence (D-001), not open source; reuse is restricted accordingly.
9. **MovieLens selection (round-2 data).** The target like rate L* comes from ratings of movies that users chose to watch, so it is a selected-exposure like rate. It is used as a stated calibration target, not as a measured like rate under random exposure (protocol section 15.4).
10. **Constructed preferences in EXP-004.** Preference vectors are derived from like counts by add-one smoothing and division by the catalog share of each genre; genres are reduced to one primary topic per movie. EXP-004 tests the simulation on these inputs, not on MovieLens users' behaviour. MovieLens users are not participants.
11. **One midpoint per tau.** The fitted midpoint is held fixed across alpha_0, eta, pool skew and drift, so like rates differ by cell.
12. **Weak drift-form check (EXP-002).** For a non-negative preference vector, additive-then-clip followed by normalisation equals the multiplicative rule with rate gamma/(1 + gamma) (0.0476 instead of 0.05) unless the clip at 1 binds. The frozen check therefore tests little more than a small change of gamma; its "not dependent on drift form" outcome (CLM-005) has limited construct value. Found after the results; the frozen check is reported as run, and a genuinely different drift form would be a new experiment.
13. **User control mixes responsiveness and slate volatility.** The random arm's value (about -0.089) shows that slate topic shares change mechanically from step to step. Arm-minus-random values (protocol 15.7) remove that reference level but do not separate responsiveness to dislikes from how much an arm's slates vary in general.
14. **Small seed-level variance.** With 200 users per run the paired differences vary little across seeds (SD about 0.005 to 0.03), so very large d_z values and tiny p-values follow even for small effects (for example popularity minus random in H_exp, -0.0089). The frozen smallest effect of interest (0.05), not significance, is the relevant threshold.
15. **Calibration changed an exploratory pattern.** Before the freeze, under the planning midpoint 0.5, the Zipf cells of the exploratory sweep showed similarity above random in H_exp (freeze record C4). Under the frozen calibration the confirmatory Zipf contrasts are strongly negative (CLM-006). The exploratory values rest on 2 seeds and another code state, but they suggest that the direction of the skew result depends on the choice-curve calibration (untested in a confirmatory run), which was fitted to a selected-exposure like rate (item 9).
16. **One calibration per experiment family.** The confirmatory results hold at the fitted midpoints; no confirmatory run varies the target like rate.
